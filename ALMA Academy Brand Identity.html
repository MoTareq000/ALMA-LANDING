01

Brand strategy

Learning to code feels like standing outside a locked room. ALMA is the door that is already open, and the guide standing in it.

The big idea

The Open Door

An arch is the oldest shape for a doorway. ALMA's silhouette is an arch with a face in it. Every learner starts by walking through it, and the arch keeps showing up as the letter A, the learning-path nodes, the badges and the app itself.

The name already says it: Adaptive Learning Mentoring Avatar. The avatar is not a mascot added to the brand. It is the brand.

Mission

Make learning to code feel like play for every student, in the language they think in, at the pace they need.

Vision

A companion that knows how you learn and grows up with you, from your first loop at 12 to your first job at 24.

Positioning

For anyone starting to code, ALMA is the adaptive companion that teaches the way you learn best, in Arabic or English. Course libraries give everyone the same lesson.

Five personality traits, one face

Playful

Wins get confetti, not certificates.

Friendly

Never talks down, never rushes.

Intelligent

Explains with pictures and steps.

Curious

Asks what happens if you try.

Encouraging

Treats mistakes as the next step.

Values

  • Start small. The first win arrives in under two minutes.
  • Mistakes are steps. Errors get a hint, never a buzzer.
  • Learn your way. Visual, hands-on, step by step or trial and error.
  • Arabic is first-class. Every screen is designed in both languages from the start.

Audience

Schoolchildren, 10 to 14

Need games, short sessions and a friendly voice.

Teenagers, 15 to 18

Need respect, real projects and no childish tone.

University first-years

Need clarity and speed on top of course material.

Adult beginners

Need patience and zero jargon at the start.

What ALMA deliberately is not

No owl or green kids-app lookNo robot headNo lightbulb or graduation capNo code brackets as decorationNo purple-blue AI gradient
02

Three creative directions

Three different ideas about what ALMA could be: a doorway you walk through, a structure you build, a path you draw.

Direction A

A · The Doorway

Story. Programming looks like a locked room. ALMA is an arch-shaped guide standing in the open door, with a face that can look at you, think, and cheer.

Logo. The arch silhouette with a window face and a floating pixel crest. The letter A of the wordmark is drawn as the same arch.

Mascot. A tall rounded arch with two pill eyes. The crest changes color to show how the learner learns best.

Type. Rounded display (Baloo 2), warm readable body (Readex Pro).

Style. Flat shapes built from arches, pills and circles. No outlines.

Adaptive link. The door and the crest adapt to each learner.

Direction B

B · Stepping Stones

Story. Learning is building. A single block with a face gains a block for every skill until the stack forms an A.

Logo. Six rounded blocks stacked into a stair-shaped A, the top block carrying the face.

Mascot. "Pip", one block that grows into a cluster as the learner levels up.

Type. Heavy geometric sans (Outfit ExtraBold) with a plain grotesque body.

Style. Bold flat blocks, high-saturation color, pixel-grid layouts.

Adaptive link. The blocks rearrange to fit the learner.

Direction C

C · One Line

Story. Every learner draws their own path. A single continuous line loops into a head and runs on toward the next goal.

Logo. One stroke that curls into a face and leaves a tail ending in a destination dot.

Mascot. "Loop", a thin-line creature that redraws itself in each pose.

Type. Light rounded sans, wide spacing, quiet tone.

Style. Monoline strokes on soft color fields, editorial feel.

Adaptive link. The path bends to follow the learner.

Recommended

Go with A, The Doorway

  • It is a face first. Two eyes and a mouth in a window can be animated for the live avatar without redesigning anything.
  • One shape runs the system. Arch becomes the mascot, the app icon, the letter A, the path nodes and the badges.
  • It survives 16 pixels. A tall orange silhouette with two dots still reads as ALMA in a browser tab.
  • The crest carries the adaptive idea. Its color shows the learning mode ALMA has picked for you.
  • Neither language owns it. The symbol has no letters, so Arabic and English sit beside it equally.

Borrowed from the others. From B, the stepping-stone blocks become the progress and puzzle visuals. From C, the looping line becomes the learning-path connector.

06

Mascot and character sheet

ALMA is an original character: a friendly arch with a screen-like window for a face. It is not a robot, a bird or an animal, and it works as a live avatar.

Front view
Three-quarter view

Anatomy

  • Arch body in Mango. The silhouette is the character.
  • Window face in Ink, always the darkest shape.
  • Pill eyes carry all emotion: shape, tilt, one or both.
  • Cheeks in Punch, only above 24 px.
  • Crest floats over the head and changes color.
  • Arms are plain pills in a deeper Mango, drawn in front.

Expressions

Happy and excited

A correct answer, a new unlock, the first run that works.

Thinking

Working out a hint, checking code, loading. Crest turns Sky.

Confused, asking

Asks the learner a question or admits it needs more detail.

Encouraging

After a wrong attempt. A wink and a raised fist: try once more.

Celebrating

Level complete, streak milestone, badge earned. Crest turns Punch.

Explaining

Lessons and walkthroughs, with a speech bubble that shows a step diagram.

Neutral idle

Waiting for input. Eyes blink every few seconds in the animated avatar.

Built to animate

Eyes, mouth, arms and crest are separate shapes on a fixed body, so a live avatar can blink, lip-sync and gesture without redrawing the character.

The crest shows how ALMA is teaching you

This is the adaptive idea made visible. ALMA watches how a learner responds and changes the crest to match the mode it chose. Tap a mode.

Step by step: short, ordered instructions with a checkmark on each one.

Mascot and logo are one drawing

The logo symbol is the mascot with the arms and feet taken away. The body, window, eyes, cheeks and crest keep the same proportions, so a learner who sees the app icon is already looking at ALMA's face.

Weights

400 for reading, 500 for labels, 700 for buttons and subheads, 800 for display only. Avoid light weights on small Arabic text.

Line spacing

Latin body 1.6. Arabic body 1.9 because the script has tall marks above and below the line. Headlines 1.05 in Latin, 1.35 in Arabic.

Rules

  • No letter-spacing on Arabic, ever.
  • No uppercase styling except tiny captions in Latin.
  • Keep lines near 65 characters.
  • Arabic runs about 8% larger than Latin beside it.

Visual language

Arches, pills, circles and stepping blocks. Programming is shown through paths, puzzles and building, not brackets.

Shapes

Arch, pill, circle, rounded square, tilted pixel. Corners are always round. Nothing has a hard point.

Illustration principles

  • Flat fills, no outlines, no gradients inside illustrations.
  • Maximum four colors per scene plus Ink.
  • Characters have eyes and nothing else on the face but a mouth.
  • Every scene has one story: where ALMA is going and what is in the way.

Mirror the interface, not the character

Progress bars, back arrows, lists and the Continue button flip in Arabic. ALMA, the logo and the wordmark never mirror.

Code stays left to right

Code blocks, variable names and terminal output keep LTR direction inside Arabic screens, with the block aligned to the reading start.

Numbers and balance

Use 0-9 for code, scores and levels. Arabic-Indic digits are fine in running prose. Size Arabic about 8% larger so its letters match Latin cap height.

Brand guidelines

The short version. Enough to build a screen, a poster or a T-shirt without asking.

Foundations

Mission

Make learning to code feel like play, in the learner's own language and pace.

Personality

Playful, friendly, intelligent, curious, encouraging. Never loud, never babyish.

Voice

Short sentences, second person, no jargon in the first lesson. "Try again" beats "Wrong".

Positioning

The adaptive coding companion for beginners, in Arabic and English.

Respect clear space

Color

  • Mango is for the next action and the character.
  • Ink for text and dark surfaces. Never pure black.
  • Deep Lagoon for links and text-bearing teal.
  • Body text on light: Ink or Slate. On dark: Chalk.
  • Check 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for icons and focus rings.

Typography

  • Baloo 2 and Baloo Bhaijaan 2 for headings and buttons.
  • Readex Pro for all reading text in both languages.
  • JetBrains Mono for code, always left to right.
  • Sentence case everywhere. No all-caps in Arabic.

Visual language

  • Arches, pills, circles, stepping blocks.
  • Flat color, four colors per scene plus Ink.
  • One mascot per screen, standing on the content.
  • Imagery: real learners at a screen, natural light, hands and faces, Arabic and English settings. No stock handshakes, no binary rain.

Digital product

One Mango action per screen. 16 px minimum body. 44 px touch targets. Hints before errors. Dark mode uses Night with unchanged Mango.

Social and presentations

One headline, one mascot pose, one color field. Titles under eight words. Slides use Ink or Chalk grounds with the symbol, not the full logo, in the corner.

Merchandise

The symbol alone on products, full logo on tags and packaging. Print in one or two spot colors: Ink plus Mango. Proof Mango on every material.
